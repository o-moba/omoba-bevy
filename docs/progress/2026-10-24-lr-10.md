# LR-10: bot route-planning CPU budget

Goal: implement [LR-10](../plans/launch-readiness.md): at most one route plan
per bot per `ROUTE_INTERVAL`, a failed plan included, and route search on
reused buffers (the static grid plus an overlay of live discs). A solo bot
arena runs 9 bots, so this is the main per-arena CPU cost before the RU host
is sized (LR-09). Branch `feat/lr-10` (omoba-bevy).

## Changes

- `common/src/bots.rs`, the movement step of the shared bot brain:
  - **Cadence.** A plan needs `now >= next_route`, and every plan sets
    `next_route = now + ROUTE_INTERVAL` (800 ms). The decisions that used to
    force an immediate plan (new target, health or gate retreat, a dash that
    moved the bot, the next lane waypoint) now only clear the route. An empty
    route at the destination (`ARRIVAL`, 0.2 m) plans nothing. So a bot plans
    at most 75 times per minute by construction.
  - **Between plans.** A bot with no route walks straight at its destination
    when the line is clear of terrain and structures
    (`segment_clear_with_discs`). The turret clip still stops it at an
    uncovered tower's range and lets it in as soon as the tower gains minion
    cover. Behind terrain or a structure, after a failed plan (`unreachable`),
    or at the end of a walked route to the same destination, it holds still.
  - The `sim_probe` route event reports `found`. A health-retreat event
    records the fraction rounded down (`fraction_below`): `hp < max_hp * line`
    can hold while the `f32` division rounds up to the line itself (198 of
    600 HP at 0.33). The simulator's `health_retreats_veteran` check failed on
    exactly that once the rounds changed.
- `shared/src/navigation/search.rs`: per-thread `Scratch` buffers. The dynamic
  overlay marks only the bounding cells of each live disc with the exact
  per-cell test (it equals the old full-grid scan; a test compares them over
  96 discs). Costs, parents and visits are stamped with a plan epoch instead of
  being reallocated; a wrapping epoch clears the stamps. The route is built in
  a reused buffer and returned as one exact copy.
- `shared/src/navigation/mod.rs`: obstacle broad-phase queries (`all_candidates`)
  reuse a per-thread index list, so point and segment checks (planning and
  bot steering) no longer allocate.
- `common/src/sim/projectiles.rs`: projectiles step in creation order. An
  intercept counts its holder's blocks, so with two shots blocked in one tick
  `HashMap` order decided which one the free block took. The new movement
  exposed this: the Elite trace's own determinism check failed in 8 of 12
  runs; after the fix 16 of 16 pass.
- Simulator (`common/tests/bot_difficulty_sim.rs`): `route_failures_per_bot_min`
  per side and a gating check `routes_within_budget_<level>` (at most 80 plans
  per bot-minute).
- Elite trace (`common/tests/fixtures/elite_bot_trace.txt`): re-baselined on
  purpose. The movement change moves Elite rounds, so the old blocks were
  stale. Two new blocks, both from commit `a42608c`: the glibc 2.39 block (CI
  runner) is taken unchanged from CI's `elite-bot-trace` artifact of
  [run 40091705808](https://github.com/o-moba/omoba-bevy/actions/runs/40091705808),
  as the re-baselining steps prescribe, and the macOS 26.6 arm64 block is
  recorded on the owner's Mac. The stale glibc 2.43 (dev VPS builder) block is
  removed until it is re-recorded there (see below); a missing block only
  skips the comparison on that `libm`.
- Tests:
  - `shared`: `disc_overlay_matches_a_full_grid_scan`,
    `reused_buffers_plan_like_fresh_ones_across_maps_and_an_epoch_wrap`, and
    `tests/navigation_alloc.rs` (a counting allocator: a warm plan through
    the A* path allocates exactly once, the returned route).
  - `common`: `a_failed_plan_is_retried_only_after_the_route_interval` (33 live
    structures make every plan fail: 5 plans in 4 s instead of 80, the bot
    holds still), `a_new_target_inside_the_route_interval_is_approached_before_the_next_plan`,
    `a_recorded_retreat_never_reads_as_the_line_it_crossed`,
    `simultaneous_blocks_follow_projectile_creation_order` (fails on the old
    `retain` loop), and both Elite trace rounds now assert that each bot's
    plans are at least `ROUTE_INTERVAL` apart and within 80 per bot-minute.
- Docs: `docs/features.md`, LR-10 boxes in `docs/plans/launch-readiness.md`,
  the S3 cost note in `docs/plans/bot-personas.md`, this note and the raw
  simulator report
  [2026-10-24-lr-10-bot-difficulty-report.json](2026-10-24-lr-10-bot-difficulty-report.json).

No player-facing strings. `GAMEPLAY_REVISION` is unchanged: bots run only on
the server online, and offline practice runs both sides in the client. Route
results for a given order are identical (the old and new search produced the
same Elite trace before the cadence change).

## Measurements

All on the owner's Mac (M4 Pro, 14 cores), release build. "Before" is
`origin/dev` 42e91d6. Its acceptance matrix is the 2026-10-14 report: rerunning
Regular and Veteran against Elite on 42e91d6 reproduced that report exactly.

Plans per bot-minute (acceptance matrix, 200 rounds per pairing, base seed 1):

| Level | Before | After | Plans that found no route (after) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Recruit | 254.1 | 10.3 | 0.00 |
| Regular | 223.4 | 10.9 | 0.00 |
| Veteran | 206.1 | 12.2 | 0.08 |
| Elite | 198.8 | 13.3 | 0.20 |

CPU per arena, single thread, the same 4 rounds (Elite against Elite twice,
Recruit against Elite twice, 10-minute cap, 40 arena-minutes):

| Step | Before (user s) | After (user s) | Gain |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 50 ms (simulator) | 11.38 | 4.47 | 2.5× |
| 10 ms (server tick) | 21.06 | 12.80 | 1.65× |

At the server's 10 ms step one core simulates about 114 bot arenas before and
about 188 after: simulation only, without networking, snapshots or the lobby.
The rest of the tick is now the larger cost; bot steering (up to 12 segment
checks per bot per tick) is the next candidate. The full matrix took 420 s of
wall time (4,689 s user) against 2,078 s in the 2026-10-14 report.

Every gating check passes (23 of 23, including the four new route-budget
checks). The ladder holds; kills, deaths, retreat lines and structures per
round move by a few percent at most.

Two variants were measured on Regular and Veteran against Elite (200 rounds
each) before settling:

- Without the clear-line walk (a bot with no route just waits), Elite pushed
  less: 7 Victories instead of 20 against Regular and a Veteran lead of −1.96.
  Bots waited at an uncovered tower's range for the next plan instead of
  walking in the moment minion cover arrived.
- Letting a new decision plan at once (breaking the one-per-interval rule)
  gave leads of −4.83 and −2.57, closest to before, at 11.8–15.6 plans per
  bot-minute. It was not kept because the spec asks for one plan per interval
  and the strict rule bounds the worst case. If the owner prefers Elite
  exactly as strong as before, this is a five-line change (decisions set
  `next_route = now` again).
